Understanding Digital Information

How images, audio, data and packets are represented, transmitted, and protected.

1. Visual Resolution

Resolution refers to how many pixels are used to form a digital image or displayed on a screen. More pixels means more visual information and a sharper picture.

Pixel Count

Resolution is expressed horizontally and vertically. Higher values → more detail.

PPI — Pixels Per Inch

Density of pixels on a display. A smaller screen with the same resolution is sharper because it has higher PPI.

DPI — Printing

Printers use dots per inch. Higher DPI produces sharper printed output, especially for photographs.

2. Audio Representation

Audio signals are transformed into digital data by measuring the wave and capturing values at regular intervals.

Sampling Rate (Hz)

Number of samples taken per second. A standard audio CD uses 44.1 kHz: 44,100 samples every second.

Bit Depth

Defines how many bits are used per sample. Higher bit depth gives better audio quality and less noise.

Waveform

Visual representation of sound amplitude over time — used to store and edit audio.

3. Quantization & Coding

To convert analog information into digital form, continuous values must be rounded and encoded into bits.

Analog → Digital

Real world signals are continuous. Digital systems store them as discrete values.

Quantization

Values are approximated to the nearest level available. More levels = higher quality.

Encoding

Digital values are stored as binary information so they can be transmitted, processed or compressed.

4. Data Transmission & Packets

Data sent across networks is divided into small packets. Each packet travels independently and is reassembled at the destination.

Header

Contains source IP, destination IP, packet size, and sequence number to rebuild data.

Payload

The actual data being transmitted — image fragment, text, audio, etc.

Trailer & CRC

Identifies the end of the packet and checks for transmission errors (Cyclic Redundancy Check).

5. Security & Data Integrity

Many techniques ensure that digital information arrives safely and remains unchanged.

Parity & Checksum

Extra bits or sums used to detect errors in data transmission.

ARQ

Automatic Repeat Request: packets are resent when errors are detected.

Encryption

Symmetric or asymmetric keys protect digital information from unauthorized access.
